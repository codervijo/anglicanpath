## Editorial standards

<Proposed>

**Written from study, not generated.** Every article is written from first-hand study of the sources it cites.

{/* VERIFY: Vijo to confirm before publishing. Both live articles were drafted with AI assistance (commits 583eb0a, 2114468), so this sentence is not yet true of them. Either rewrite the sentence to describe the process as it actually is, or rewrite the articles until it is true. */}

**Every claim cites a primary source.** Each date, figure, quotation, and description of what a church teaches traces to a source listed at the foot of the article. We prefer primary sources — the prayer books themselves, the Articles of Religion, the canons and official statements of the churches concerned. Where we rely on scholarship instead, the source list says so.

**Fair to other traditions.** When an article compares Anglicanism with another tradition, it aims to describe that tradition accurately enough that a thoughtful member of it would call the description fair, even where they disagree with our conclusions. Where an article gives our own view, it says that the view is ours.

**Corrections are prompt and dated.** When we find or are told of an error, we correct it promptly. The article's "Updated" date changes, and a correction that changes what the article says is described in a dated note on the article. To report an error, write to the address under [Contact](#contact).

</Proposed>

## Review process

<Proposed>

Each article is reviewed by a named priest after it has been written, sourced and fact-checked. Until that happens, the article says so in its byline: "Not yet reviewed by clergy."

- **A reviewer reads a finished article**, not a draft, and reviews it for doctrinal and liturgical accuracy.
- **The signature belongs to that version.** A reviewed article shows the reviewer's name and title and the date they read it.
- **A substantive edit removes the signature.** If the article is later changed in what it says — rather than a typo fixed or a link repaired — the reviewer's name comes off, and the byline returns to "Not yet reviewed by clergy" until the reviewer has read the new version. When in doubt, we treat a change as substantive.
- **Reviewers may add a note in their own words**, shown beside their signature. We do not edit it.
- **A signature is not an endorsement of every word**, nor of other articles, nor of the site as a whole.

If you are a priest and would consider reviewing, [here is what it involves](/about/review/).

</Proposed>

## Independence and funding

<Proposed>

- **No advertising and no paywall.** Every page is free to read, and nothing on the site is sponsored.
- **Not for profit.** Anglican Path is run as a not-for-profit resource.
- **Not officially affiliated with any jurisdiction.** Anglican Path is not an official publication of the ACNA, of any Continuing Anglican church, or of any diocese or parish. Clergy who review articles do so personally; a signature does not speak for the reviewer's jurisdiction.

</Proposed>
